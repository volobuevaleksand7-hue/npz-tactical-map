confirmed Middle East · 20 November 2026

Tanker hit by projectile and set on fire 8 nautical miles north of Khasab in Strait of Hormuz

On 20 November UKMTO reported that a tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile about 8 nautical miles north of Oman's Khasab; a fire broke out on the port bow, and according to the vessel's company there were no casualties or environmental impact. Iran's IRGC later said the tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine, Iranian state television reported; the vessel's name was not disclosed. According to analyst Martin Kelly of Orbital Risk Intelligence, 15 attacks and piracy-related incidents were recorded over the past seven days, 50% more than the previous week.

Effect on fuel. According to UKMTO, 115 outbound and 148 inbound transits were recorded through the strait over the week, against 139 and 169 the week before; combined traffic remains about 65% below pre-conflict levels.

confirmed Middle East · 20 November 2026

Major container carriers return to the Suez Canal

On 20 November Riviera Maritime Media reported that Ocean Network Express had resumed transits through the Suez Canal, while Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd announced on 16 November that they would move three more Gemini Cooperation services from the Cape of Good Hope route to Suez. The transition will be phased between December 2026 and January 2027; according to Alphaliner, 63% of Far East-Europe container capacity was deployed on services using the Red Sea route between mid-September and mid-October.

Effect on fuel. The return of container ships to the shorter Suez route changes the structure of bunker demand: the voyage is shorter than the Cape of Good Hope detour, which reduces fuel consumption per voyage.

confirmed East Asia · 19 November 2026

US, China and Russia agree at Shenzhen summit to expand refined product output and supplies

On 19 November, following a trilateral summit in Shenzhen, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin said in a joint statement that they plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products and reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows. The statement set no volume targets or timelines. Analysts interviewed by Al Jazeera doubt the pledge can be delivered: US refineries are running near full capacity, Russian refining has been cut by drone strikes, and China's crude imports are constrained by disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz; according to Rystad Energy, European gasoil prices fell 1.5% after the announcement while crude barely moved.

Effect on fuel. According to Rystad Energy, the statement contains no volume targets or timetable, so no significant increase in refined product supply is expected in the near term.

China's crude imports from the Persian Gulf rose 42.4% in October

On 20 November Chinese customs data analysed by the South China Morning Post showed that China imported 19.7 million tonnes of crude from Persian Gulf producers (excluding Iran) in October, 42.4% more than in September and the highest level since the war began. According to the publication, Gulf shipments accounted for almost half of China's term supplies, approaching pre-war levels. Saudi Aramco cut the December price of Arab Light for Asia by $2 to a discount of $6.50 a barrel to the Oman/Dubai average, the widest since May 2020.

Effect on fuel. Higher purchases and producer discounts support Chinese refinery runs and increase demand for tankers in the Gulf.

reported, single source South Asia · 20 November 2026

Indian refiners lock in tankers on long-term charters as Gulf disruptions persist

On 20 November Bloomberg reported that Indian refiners are expanding long-term tanker charters: Reliance Industries increased the number of supertankers on time charter to about a dozen from seven to eight previously, while state-owned processors are seeking contracts running months or years but had not signed new deals as of last week. According to the report, suppliers increasingly sell crude on a delivered basis and freight costs have risen to historic highs, prompting buyers to lock in vessels at fixed rates. India imports more than 90% of its crude and has little tanker capacity of its own.

Effect on fuel. Locking in vessels reduces Indian refiners' exposure to spot freight rates that have risen on attacks in the Strait of Hormuz; the impact on supply volumes has not been assessed.

Japan to buy up to 8 million barrels of Saudi crude for reserves and expand storage for Aramco

On 20 November Japan and Saudi Arabia announced a deal under which Tokyo plans to buy up to 8 million barrels of Saudi crude for its national petroleum reserve, part of which was drawn down during the Strait of Hormuz crisis; the purchases will be made subject to favourable market conditions. The agreement also gives Saudi Aramco temporary access to an additional 16 million barrels of storage capacity in Japan on top of the 8 million barrels it already holds there; Bloomberg reported that the company has begun refilling the tanks. Last month Japan released part of its reserves as part of the IEA-coordinated release of 400 million barrels.

Effect on fuel. The return of Saudi crude to Japanese tanks restores a buffer for Asian refineries after the stock release, but purchases are capped at 8 million barrels.

Iran masses more than 14 tankers at Kharg Island in attempt to break US naval blockade

On 20 November Euronews, citing satellite imagery and ship-tracking data, reported that Iran had massed more than 14 oil tankers at Kharg Island and planned to send them in convoy toward the Sea of Oman exit in the coming days. According to TankerTrackers, available data confirm that Iran is resuming oil exports; media had earlier reported a ship-to-ship transfer of Iranian cargo in the Persian Gulf with AIS switched off. Israel's Channel 14 and Sabereen News, an outlet close to Iran-backed groups, reported that dozens of tankers were preparing to head toward China; there is no independent confirmation of those reports.

Effect on fuel. The convoy preparations and reports of resumed exports point to a possible change in the volume of Iranian supplies through the Strait of Hormuz, but actual flows are not confirmed by independent data.

US Treasury presses Singapore and Malaysia to tighten enforcement of Iran sanctions

On 20 November Reuters reported that US Treasury Department officials met government authorities in Singapore and Malaysia this week, including regulators and national security bodies, and warned them of risks from non-compliance with sanctions on Iran. According to a Treasury official, Washington is seeking additional scrutiny of maritime transport, aviation services and procurement networks to ensure they are not fronting for Iran. No new sanctions or specific measures were reported.

Effect on fuel. According to Reuters, ship-to-ship transfers of Iranian oil in the waters off Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia have regularly been used to obscure the cargo's origin; tighter enforcement affects that route.

reported, single source Africa · 18 November 2026

Multi-hour queues for cooking gas in Tripoli

On 18 November TVC News reported that residents of Tripoli were spending two to three hours in queues for cooking gas amid a prolonged shortage. According to the state-owned Brega Petroleum Marketing Company, the shortage was caused by disruptions to tanker movements and their arrival schedules at Libyan ports; the company said it resumed gas pumping from the tanker Derna on Saturday to replenish reserves.

Effect on fuel. Disruptions to seaborne LPG supplies have caused a shortage of household fuel in Libya.
