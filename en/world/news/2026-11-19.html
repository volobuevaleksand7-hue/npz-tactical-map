confirmed Middle East · 18 November 2026

Bahrain's Sitra refinery running above 80% after six strikes

On November 18 Bapco Energies chief executive Mark Thomas said at a conference in Dubai that the 415,000 barrels-per-day Sitra refinery is running above 80% of capacity and will reach full output by the end of the year, with hydrocrackers the last units to restart. According to him, the plant has been hit six times by Iranian strikes since late February. About 80% of output is now consumed in the Gulf, whereas before the war 85% was exported through the Strait of Hormuz.

Effect on fuel. Naphtha and LPG still leave through the Strait of Hormuz at charter rates four times normal and $17 million in insurance per transit, according to Bapco.

EU gas storage at start of heating season lowest in a decade

On November 18 EU Perspectives, citing Gas Infrastructure Europe, reported that EU underground gas storage was 72.65% full on November 8; the IEA estimates stocks at the start of the heating season were the lowest since 2013. The European Commission said there is no immediate threat to supply and member states are not reporting disruptions. According to ACER, refilling storage in 2026 cost up to 27 billion euros.

Effect on fuel. Low stocks increase Europe's reliance on LNG imports and the risk of price volatility during the heating season, according to the IEA.

Equinor reports cyberattack on IT systems; output unaffected

On 17 November Equinor reported a cyberattack on part of its corporate IT systems. According to The Record, employees had problems accessing applications and internal email was down. The company said oil and gas production was not affected and access to production systems was retained; no group claimed responsibility.

Effect on fuel. Equinor is Europe's largest pipeline gas supplier; according to the company, production and supplies were not interrupted.

ADNOC L&S vessel attacked in Strait of Hormuz: 11 injured, one crew member missing

On November 17 a vessel of ADNOC Logistics & Services was attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz; in a statement on November 18 the company said 11 crew members were injured, one remains missing, the others were evacuated and search and rescue operations continue. Iraq resumes oil exports via Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline

On November 17 Iraq resumed pumping crude for export through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline to Turkey, which had been idle for more than a decade, Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair said. Initial capacity is 120,000 barrels per day, with plans to gradually raise it to 500,000 barrels per day. According to the minister, the route provides an additional export channel for oil from northern fields and reduces reliance on southern terminals and the Strait of Hormuz.

Effect on fuel. The restart adds up to 120,000 barrels per day of Iraqi exports outside the Strait of Hormuz, with plans to raise the volume to 500,000 barrels per day according to the ministry.

U.S. strikes IRGC launch site on Qeshm Island

On November 17 a U.S. fighter jet used precision munitions against a launch site of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, a U.S. military official said on condition of anonymity. India raises export levies on diesel and jet fuel

On November 16 the Indian government raised the special additional excise duty on diesel exports from 11.5 to 14 rupees per litre and on aviation turbine fuel exports from 11 to 12.5 rupees per litre, keeping the rate for petrol at zero. Iran expects winter gas deficit of about 430 million cubic metres per day

On November 16 Al Jazeera, citing a government forecast, reported that Iran's peak winter natural gas deficit could reach about 430 million cubic metres per day and affect households and industry. Saeed Tavakoli, head of the National Iranian Gas Company, said 26 projectiles struck six gas processing plants on March 18, knocking out 28% of gas production capacity; about 100 million cubic metres per day of capacity has been restored, and full restoration of the remaining 130 million cubic metres may extend to 2028. Gas imports to close the gap are unavailable, according to Hashem Oraee of the Iranian Energy Associations Union.

Effect on fuel. The gas deficit forces power plants to burn mazut; deputy energy minister Mostafa Rajabi Mashhadi confirmed that mazut burning will continue this year as well.

Effect on fuel. Retail petrol and diesel prices in Pakistan fall for a second consecutive week, tracking global benchmarks.

CENTCOM extends blockade rules to ship-to-ship transfers of Iranian oil

On 18 November CENTCOM issued an update to its advisory on the blockade of Iranian ports: a ship receiving cargo via ship-to-ship transfer from a vessel loaded in Iran may be considered to be evading the blockade and may be stopped, boarded, captured or directed to an Iranian port. According to Reuters, the blockade was first imposed on 13 April, lifted in June and reimposed on 14 July; neutral transit through the Strait of Hormuz to non-Iranian destinations is not impeded. Iran has exported some LPG and jet fuel since July, according to Reuters sources.

Effect on fuel. The measure is intended to curb exports of Iranian refined products through transfers at sea; no quantitative estimate of the effect is given.

US LNG tankers diverted to Europe

According to ship-tracking data from Bloomberg and Kpler, at least two tankers carrying US LNG that were heading to Egypt changed course toward Europe, while two other vessels had earlier left the Asian route. Bloomberg reported that the diversions add supply to the European market and curb prices while disruptions in the Middle East keep the market tight. An increase in LNG flows from Qatar was also noted.

Effect on fuel. Additional LNG cargoes support European supply and limit price gains; the global balance remains tight.
