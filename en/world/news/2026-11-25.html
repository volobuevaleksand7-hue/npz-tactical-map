confirmed Latin America · 24 November 2026

WHO chief calls for lifting US fuel restrictions on Cuba

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called in Havana on November 24 for the lifting of US fuel restrictions on Cuba, saying they harm the population and the healthcare system. The WHO will provide $2 million for an immunization program and medical equipment; Cuban authorities link the restrictions to power outages of up to 20 hours a day.

Effect on fuel. According to Cuban authorities, the fuel restrictions have led to power outages of up to 20 hours a day and disruptions to infrastructure operations.

confirmed Middle East · 24 November 2026

Iraqi prime minister orders federal control of oilfields in areas under Kurdish control

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the Oil Ministry on November 24 to manage oilfields in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh provinces that are under the protection of Kurdish forces. He also ordered the closure of unofficial border crossings and allowed crude oil and petroleum product exports only through the federal ministry; the Kurdish side rejected the decision.

Effect on fuel. Publications note the risk of a deepening dispute between Baghdad and Erbil over oil exports; no immediate impact on shipments has been recorded.

confirmed Europe · 23 November 2026

European Commission proposes 22nd sanctions package banning Russia-insured tankers and services for refineries

The European Commission presented its 22nd sanctions package against Russia on November 23. The package provides for a ban on tankers insured in Russia and on European companies' services for the construction, operation and repair of Russian energy facilities, including refineries; more than 70 vessels were added to the теневой флот list. Adoption requires unanimous approval from all 27 EU member states.

Effect on fuel. According to S&P Global, the immediate effect of the restrictions will be limited because European contractors left the Russian refining sector after 2022.

reported, single source Middle East · 24 November 2026

Iran adds 26 tugboats to Strait of Hormuz blacklist; list now has 266 vessels

The Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA) added 26 tugboats on November 24 to its list of vessels barred from transiting the Strait of Hormuz without Iranian permission, the semi-official Tasnim news agency reported. The list now contains 266 vessels; the authority had earlier warned that companies servicing tankers would also be added.

Effect on fuel. Tugboats are used to tow damaged tankers and conduct salvage operations, so the restrictions complicate vessel servicing in the strait, publications report.
