Saudi-led coalition strikes Hodeidah port after Riyadh attack

On 25 November the Saudi-led coalition said it struck Houthi "operational capabilities" at Hodeidah port and targets in Saada and Amran provinces after warning civilians to leave the port. According to The National, the port handles 80% of Yemen's humanitarian aid and 70% of its commercial imports. The strikes followed Houthi attacks on Saudi sites, including a hit on a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District on 24 November that injured 11 people.

Effect on fuel. Sources do not report a direct effect on global fuel flows; the port handles 70% of Yemen's commercial imports.

US-led consortium agrees to buy Lukoil's foreign assets

On 25 November a consortium led by US firm Eldridge (Todd Boehly) said it had agreed with Lukoil to swap securities for the company's non-Russian oil and gas assets, including assets in Bulgaria, Romania, Africa and the Middle East. The group includes the US International Development Finance Corporation, UAE-based Catena Investment Holding and Xtellus Partners; the deal is expected to be reviewed by the US Treasury's OFAC, which sanctioned Lukoil in October 2025.

Effect on fuel. The deal changes ownership of Lukoil's foreign refining and marketing assets, including its Bulgarian assets; no immediate effect on current fuel flows has been recorded.

confirmed Europe · 25 November 2026

OPEC+ to set 2027 quotas on 29 November; Iraq and Kazakhstan dispute capacity review

On 29 November OPEC+ ministers will meet to set production policy for 2027; according to Bloomberg, this is the alliance's most consequential decision this year after the UAE's exit and amid quota disputes. The talks will rely on the first external audit of members' capacities by consultant DeGolyer and MacNaughton; Iraq disputes an estimate of 4.9 million bpd and seeks recognition of about 6 million bpd, while Kazakhstan has also asked for a higher rating, Reuters reported.

Effect on fuel. The quotas will determine OPEC+ oil supply in 2027; traders are concerned about a surplus as Gulf flows recover, which has weighed on prices.

confirmed Europe · 25 November 2026

UK energy bills to rise 26% from 1 January

On 25 November regulator Ofgem said the household energy price cap will rise 26% from 1 January, taking the typical annual bill to £2,166 ($2,845), up £443. Ofgem links the increase to higher wholesale gas prices caused by the Middle East conflict; more than 20 million households on variable tariffs are affected.

Effect on fuel. The increase is driven by higher wholesale gas prices amid the Middle East conflict and disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, adding pressure on European gas markets.

Malawi fuel shortage deepens with queues at filling stations

In November 2026 Malawi's petrol and diesel shortage deepened: in Lilongwe and Blantyre drivers spend hours and nights queuing at filling stations, Bizna Kenya reported, citing an Al Jazeera report of 20 November. The cause is a shortage of foreign currency to pay for petroleum imports; confrontations at filling stations were also reported.

Effect on fuel. A foreign-currency shortage limits purchases of imported fuel, disrupting station supplies and raising transport costs.

Petronas expects tight LNG market to persist in 2027

On 25 November Petronas forecast that the global LNG market will remain relatively tight next year as supply disruptions in the Middle East continue; a company official said this could keep LNG prices elevated until more supply becomes available. According to OilPrice, Qatar is struggling to restore LNG flows, while US LNG exports are running at record highs.

Effect on fuel. A tight LNG balance keeps gas prices elevated in Asia and Europe until new capacity comes online.

reported, single source Middle East · 25 November 2026

Houthis claim strike on Ras Tanura refinery; Saudi Arabia reports downing drones

On 25 November the Houthis claimed a strike on the 550,000 bpd Ras Tanura refinery and said fires broke out at the site. The Saudi-led coalition said air defences destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura, that fragments fell without casualties, and did not confirm the refinery was hit. Satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters showed thick smoke over the refinery area; Saudi Aramco and the Saudi government did not respond to a request for comment.

Effect on fuel. After the reports Brent rose about 2.4% to $93.60 a barrel; Ras Tanura remains a major Saudi export terminal.
