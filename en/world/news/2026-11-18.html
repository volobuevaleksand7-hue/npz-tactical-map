confirmed Europe · 17 November 2026

OPEC+ completes first independent assessment of members' production capacity

On November 17 OPEC announced the completion of the first independent assessment of the maximum sustainable production capacities of OPEC members and partners in the Declaration of Cooperation; DeGolyer and MacNaughton assessed most countries, and India's Shri Chandra assessed Russia and Venezuela. Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais called the result a milestone for the OPEC+ framework; the findings will underpin quotas for 2027 and beyond. Country-level figures were not disclosed.

Effect on fuel. The assessment sets the baseline for 2027 quotas; according to Reuters, about 2 million barrels per day of OPEC+ cuts remain in place through the end of 2026.

Philippines and Japan sign agreement on strategic fuel reserves

On November 17 the Philippine Department of Energy announced the signing of a memorandum of cooperation with Japan on developing the Philippines' strategic petroleum reserves. At the ASEAN Summit and the AZEC summit in Manila, Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi proposed making unused Japanese oil storage tanks available for a Philippine-led regional stockpiling programme. Volumes and timelines were not disclosed.

Effect on fuel. The initiative is aimed at building regional emergency fuel reserves amid supply disruptions caused by the Middle East conflict.

Four ASEAN countries agree on regional oil stockpiling

On 16 November at the ASEAN summit in Manila, Philippine Trade Secretary Cristina Roque said Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines and Singapore had agreed to a proposal for regional oil stockpiles as part of reviving the APSA agreement; the UAE has also expressed interest in taking part. A pilot scheme envisages the Philippines drawing on storage in Indonesia and Thailand under a ticketing arrangement, with no start date given.

Effect on fuel. Regional stockpiles are intended to cut logistics costs and fuel delivery times in Southeast Asia during supply disruptions.

confirmed North America · 16 November 2026

US Strategic Petroleum Reserve falls to lowest level since 1982

According to the US Department of Energy, crude stocks in the Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to 276.4 million barrels last week, the lowest level since August 1982. The decline is part of a plan to release about 172 million barrels after 32 IEA member countries agreed in March to make 400 million barrels of emergency reserves available to offset supply disruptions caused by the Middle East war.

Effect on fuel. The reserve release is intended to offset oil supply disruptions caused by the Middle East conflict.

LPG tanker GAS HUSKY sinks off Bulgaria with 3,400 tonnes of liquefied gas

On November 17 an LPG tanker GAS HUSKY, sailing under the Liberian flag with a cargo of propane from Egypt, sank inside Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone; according to Bulgaria's defence ministry, the vessel was struck by drones about 55 nautical miles southeast of Varna. All 19 crew members, Philippine nationals, were rescued from a life raft and taken to an air base in Varna, and no pollution was recorded. The cause of the sinking and responsibility have not been established.

Effect on fuel. The vessel was carrying 3,400 tonnes of liquefied gas; the sinking came a day after a cargo ship went down in the same area, raising risks for maritime logistics in the western Black Sea.

reported, single source Middle East · 17 November 2026

Tankertrackers: capacity of empty tankers heading to the Persian Gulf down about 40%

On November 17 Bloomberg, citing vessel-tracking service Tankertrackers.com, reported that the combined capacity of empty VLCCs and Suezmaxes sailing to load Middle East crude has fallen by about 40% since the end of August. Some vessels avoid the Strait of Hormuz, with cargoes transferred in the Gulf of Oman, where there are not enough vacant tankers, according to Tankertrackers.com co-founder Samir Madani. The estimate is based on vessel-movement tracking.

Effect on fuel. The shortage of empty tankers could limit the recovery of oil export flows from the region, the source said.

Iraqi PM to visit Lebanon to discuss oil export routes bypassing Hormuz

On November 17 Al Jazeera reported that Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi is preparing to visit Beirut, with talks expected on Wednesday. The agenda includes trade, customs exemptions, security coordination and a proposed pipeline from Kirkuk through Syria's Baniyas to Tripoli in Lebanon. The project is seen as a possible way to bypass the Strait of Hormuz, where shipments have been disrupted since the start of the US-Israeli war with Iran. No decisions on the project have been announced.

Effect on fuel. The discussion of an alternative route for Iraqi oil exports is linked to disruptions to shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.

Japan proposes an "Asian IEA" to manage oil stockpiles

On 17 November Japan proposed at the ASEAN summit a joint Asian framework for managing crude oil stockpiles and a "Power Gulf" initiative to expand cooperation between Asian importers and Middle East suppliers. According to OilPrice, the proposal came amid supply disruptions caused by the conflict around Iran; details of the mechanism and timelines were not disclosed.

Effect on fuel. A shared stockpile system could reduce the impact of supply disruptions on Asian importers, but no specific volume commitments were announced.

reported, single source Africa · 15 November 2026

Hours-long queues at Khartoum filling stations amid fuel shortage

On 15 November Africanews reported that drivers in Khartoum were waiting several hours at the few filling stations still operating; it cited damaged energy infrastructure and the loss of domestic refining capacity, which has left Sudan dependent on imported petroleum products. According to a Trends Africa report on 17 November, the situation is aggravated by rising world prices and the depreciation of the Sudanese pound.

Effect on fuel. The fuel shortage raises transport and basic goods costs in Sudan.
